What is the tax in Quebec?

Sales tax, what it applies to, and the other taxes you'll run into in Quebec in 2026.

Updated November 2026

Quick answer

The sales tax in Quebec is 14.975%: the 5% GST (federal) plus the 9.975% QST (Quebec Sales Tax). Both are calculated on the price before tax, so a $100 item costs $114.98. Multiply a price by 1.14975 to get the total; divide a total by 1.14975 to remove the tax.

The two sales taxes in Quebec

Quebec charges two separate sales taxes on most goods and services. The GST (Goods and Services Tax) is the 5% federal tax charged across Canada. The QST (Quebec Sales Tax, TVQ in French) is the 9.975% provincial tax collected by Revenu Québec. On a receipt they appear as two lines, often labelled TPS and TVQ.

Both taxes are calculated on the price before tax: the QST is not charged on the GST. That's why the combined rate is simply 5% + 9.975% = 14.975%. Quebec does not use HST.

What isn't taxed in Quebec?

Some items are zero-rated (taxable at 0%) or exempt, so no GST or QST is added. The most common:

  • Basic groceries: fresh fruit and vegetables, meat, milk, bread, eggs (but not snacks, soft drinks, candy or restaurant meals)
  • Prescription drugs and many medical devices
  • Residential rent and most child care
  • Most health care and educational services
  • The sale of a used (resale) home

Are prices in Quebec shown with tax included?

Usually not. Store shelves, menus and online prices are almost always shown before tax, and the 14.975% is added at the checkout. Two common exceptions: gasoline prices at the pump and SAQ (liquor store) prices already include tax.

Do you tip on the amount before or after tax?

In Quebec restaurants, tips of 15% to 20% are customary. Many people tip on the amount before tax. A handy shortcut: the two tax lines on your bill add up to almost exactly 15%, so tipping the same amount as the taxes is a 15% tip.

Can tourists get the tax back?

No. Canada ended its GST visitor rebate program in 2007, and Quebec does not refund the QST to visitors. Tourists pay the same 14.975% as residents on purchases in Quebec. (Businesses registered for GST/QST can claim input tax credits, which is a different system.)

Other taxes in Quebec

  • Income tax: Quebec has its own income tax, from 14% to 25.75% in 2026, on top of federal tax (reduced by 16.5% for Quebec residents). See the 2026 Quebec tax brackets or the income tax calculator.
  • Payroll deductions: QPP, EI and QPIP also come off each paycheque. The Quebec paycheck calculator shows your take-home pay.
  • Welcome tax: buyers of a home pay transfer duties to the municipality, from 0.5% to 1.5% and more in Montreal. See the welcome tax calculator.
  • Specific taxes: fuel, alcohol and tobacco carry extra taxes that are built into the price, and hotels add a lodging tax.

Frequently asked questions

What is the tax in Quebec?

14.975% in total: the 5% federal GST plus the 9.975% Quebec Sales Tax (QST), both calculated on the price before tax.

How much is tax on $100 in Quebec?

$14.98: $5.00 of GST and $9.98 of QST, for a total of $114.98.

What is the QST?

The Quebec Sales Tax (TVQ in French), a 9.975% provincial sales tax collected by Revenu Québec on most goods and services, in addition to the 5% GST.

Does Quebec have HST?

No. Quebec charges the GST and the QST separately. HST is used in Ontario and the Atlantic provinces.

Is Quebec sales tax the highest in Canada?

Almost. At 14.975%, Quebec is just below the 15% HST of New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador and Prince Edward Island, and above Ontario (13%), BC and Manitoba (12%), Saskatchewan (11%) and Alberta (5%).

Do tourists get sales tax back in Quebec?

No. The federal visitor rebate program ended in 2007 and Quebec does not refund the QST to visitors.

Is tax included in prices in Quebec?

Usually not: most prices are shown before tax and the 14.975% is added at checkout. Gas at the pump and SAQ prices include tax.
