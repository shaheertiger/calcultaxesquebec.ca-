What comes off each paycheque

  • Federal income tax, less the 16.5% Quebec abatement
  • Quebec income tax — brackets from 14% to 25.75%
  • QPP: 6.3% between $3,500 and $74,600, then 4% up to $85,000
  • Employment Insurance: 1.30% up to $68,900
  • QPIP: 0.430% up to $103,000

Frequently asked questions

How much is a $50,000 salary every two weeks in Quebec?

About $1,510.14 net every two weeks in 2026, or $3,271.97 a month, after federal and Quebec tax, QPP, EI and QPIP.

How much is $25 an hour after tax in Quebec?

At 40 hours a week, $25/h is $52,000 a year gross and about $1,562.71 net every two weeks, or $19.53 net per hour.

What is deducted from a Quebec paycheque?

Federal income tax, Quebec income tax, the Quebec Pension Plan (QPP), Employment Insurance at Quebec's reduced rate, and the Quebec Parental Insurance Plan (QPIP). Employers may also deduct union dues, pension or group insurance.

Why is my actual paycheque slightly different?

Employers use payroll withholding tables and your own TD1 / TP-1015.3 forms. QPP and EI also stop once the annual maximum is reached, so late-year paycheques can be higher.

Why is take-home pay lower in Quebec than in Ontario?

Quebec's provincial income tax is higher, partly offset by the 16.5% federal abatement and lower EI. In exchange, Quebec funds programs like subsidized daycare and QPIP parental leave.
