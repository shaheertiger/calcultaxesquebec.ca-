Quick answer

In 2026, Quebec's welcome tax is 0.5% up to $62,900, 1% up to $315,000 and 1.5% above. On a $500,000 home that's $5,610.50. First-time buyers can get up to $5,875 back.

2026 brackets

  • 0.5% on the portion up to $62,900
  • 1% from $62,900 to $315,000
  • 1.5% above $315,000 (provincial scale)
  • Montreal: 2% from $552,300 to $1,104,700, 2.5% to $2,136,500, 3.5% to $3,113,000, 4% above
  • Quebec City: 2.5% from $500,000 to $750,000, 3% above

Sources: Québec.ca (transfer duties), montreal.ca, ville.quebec.qc.ca. Other cities such as Laval may also charge higher rates above $500,000; check with your municipality. In 2027 the brackets rise to $64,400 and $322,600.

Frequently asked questions

How is the welcome tax calculated in Quebec?

In 2026: 0.5% on the first $62,900, 1% from $62,900 to $315,000 and 1.5% above, applied to the higher of the purchase price or the assessed value.

How much is the welcome tax on a $500,000 house?

About $5,611 on the 2026 provincial scale. Montreal and Quebec City give the same amount at $500,000 because their higher rates start above $552,300 and $500,000.

Do first-time buyers get the welcome tax back?

Since 2026, a refundable tax credit repays 100% of the first $5,000 of duties and 25% of the next $3,500, up to $5,875, for homes under $1 million.

When do you pay the welcome tax?

The municipality sends the bill a few weeks or months after the deed is registered; it is usually due within 30 days.

Is the welcome tax the same as land transfer tax?

Yes. "Welcome tax" is the common name for Quebec's duties on transfers of immovables (droits de mutation), paid to the municipality.
