2026 Quebec payroll contributions

  • QPP: 6.3% on earnings between $3,500 and $74,600, then 4% up to $85,000
  • Employment Insurance: 1.30% up to $68,900 (Quebec's reduced rate)
  • QPIP: 0.430% up to $103,000
  • Federal tax: brackets from 14% to 33%, less the 16.5% Quebec abatement
  • Quebec tax: brackets from 14% to 25.75%

What the estimate leaves out

It assumes employment income only and basic personal credits. RRSP or pension contributions, taxable benefits, family credits and other income change the result. For official figures, see Revenu Québec and the Canada Revenue Agency.

Frequently asked questions

How much tax do I pay on $60,000 in Quebec?

On a $60,000 salary in 2026, total deductions (federal and Quebec income tax, QPP, EI and QPIP) are about $14,143, leaving roughly $45,857 a year, or $3,821 a month.

Why does Quebec have two income tax returns?

Quebec collects its own provincial income tax through Revenu Québec, so residents file a federal return with the CRA and a provincial return (TP-1) with Revenu Québec.

What is the Quebec abatement?

Quebec residents get a 16.5% reduction of basic federal tax, because Quebec funds some programs that the federal government funds elsewhere.

What payroll deductions come off a Quebec paycheque?

Federal income tax, Quebec income tax, the Quebec Pension Plan (QPP), Employment Insurance at Quebec's reduced rate, and the Quebec Parental Insurance Plan (QPIP).

Is this calculator exact?

It is an estimate for a single salaried employee with basic credits. RRSP contributions, pension plans, taxable benefits and family credits change the real amount.
